# Papua New Guinea — Implementation of 2021 (3rd cycle) UPR Recommendations

Assessment of Papua New Guinea's implementation of the 161 recommendations received in the 3rd UPR cycle (A/HRC/49/11, 39th session, 2021-11-04), using the Human Rights Committee A–E grading system (CCPR/C/108/2). Evidence is the 4th-cycle pre-sessional documentation (November 2026 (session 53) review).

**Sources**

- **National Report** — A/HRC/WG.6/53/PNG/1
- **UN Compilation** — A/HRC/WG.6/53/PNG/2, OHCHR
- **Stakeholders' Summary** — A/HRC/WG.6/53/PNG/3, OHCHR

Grades: **A** largely satisfactory · **B** partially satisfactory · **C** not satisfactory / no relevant action · **D** no information · **E** measures contrary to, or rejection of, the recommendation.

This is a **first-pass assessment for expert review**. Recommendations are grouped by their OHCHR theme; within a theme, near-identical asks share a grade, with separate blocks where the substance (and grade) differ. Each block notes the position Papua New Guinea took at the review (supported / noted / supported-noted); a *noted* recommendation was not accepted.

## Summary

| Grade | Count | Share |
|---|---:|---:|
| **A** — Largely implemented / action largely satisfactory | 16 | 10% |
| **B** — Partially implemented / steps taken, further action needed | 96 | 60% |
| **C** — Not implemented / no relevant action taken | 48 | 30% |
| **D** — No information available on implementation | 1 | 1% |
| **Total** | 161 | 100% |

**Partial implementation (B)** is the dominant outcome, and Papua New Guinea gives a detailed National Report: the death penalty was abolished in 2022-2023, the Disability Services Act 2025 was enacted, a costed GBV Strategy 2026-2035 and a sorcery-accusation action plan (2024-2029) were launched, the Strategic Plan on Human Rights 2024-2029 and a National Human Rights Policy (2026) were adopted, a 10% women-candidate quota was legislated for 2027, and health and education funding rose. **No relevant action (C)** persists on ratification of CAT, OP-CAT and ICPPED, a comprehensive anti-discrimination law, decriminalisation of same-sex relations, the minimum ages of marriage and criminal responsibility, an access-to-information law, a national action plan on business and human rights and an established national human rights institution (draft legislation only). Reported on-the-ground violence, forced evictions and pressure on journalists remain serious concerns.
