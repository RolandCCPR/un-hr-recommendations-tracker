I. Process for the preparation of the report
1. The present report is submitted pursuant to Human Rights Council decision 17/119 and the updated guidelines of the Office of the United Nations High Commissioner for Human Rights (OHCHR) for the fourth cycle of the universal periodic review.
2. Representatives from more than 25 ministries and departments participated in the preparation of the report. The preparation process was led by the Government Commission on Compliance with International Human Rights Obligations, with the Human Rights Guarantees Department in the Executive Office of the President, acting as its secretariat.
3. This report draws on disaggregated statistical data from the Statistics Agency under the Office of the President, including the findings of the 2023 demographic and health survey and the Agency’s dedicated publication, Children of Tajikistan (2025).
4. Broad national consultations were held during the preparation of the report. This report was prepared taking into account the proposals submitted by civil society organizations, the national human rights institution and international partners during the national consultations on the universal periodic review that were held on 16 July 2026. The consultations provided an opportunity for open discussion of the draft national report, an exchange of views on the implementation of the recommendations from the previous review cycle and the formulation of proposals aimed at enhancing the completeness, objectivity and inclusiveness of the information presented.
5. On 30 July 2026, the draft report was considered and approved at a meeting of the Government Commission on International Human Rights Obligations for subsequent submission to the Council.
II. Voluntary commitments
6. In 2022, Tajikistan submitted a voluntary national report for the midterm review of the implementation of the Sendai Framework for Disaster Risk Reduction 2015–2030.1
7. Tajikistan submitted its fourth national communication on the implementation of the United Nations Framework Convention on Climate Change in 2022.2
8. In July 2023, Tajikistan submitted its second voluntary national review on the implementation of the Sustainable Development Goals, prepared with the support of the United Nations system. The review was prepared in accordance with the principles of inclusiveness and broad participation, with particular attention paid to engaging the most vulnerable groups and ensuring that no one is left behind.
III. National human rights institutions
9. The Office of the Commissioner for Human Rights (Ombudsman) has been accredited with category B status; however, the reforms undertaken are bringing it closer to full compliance with international standards.
10. With the adoption of the Equality and Elimination of All Forms of Discrimination Act on 19 July 2022, the Office of the Commissioner for Human Rights was designated as the competent State body in this area. In 2024, corresponding amendments were made to the Act on the Commissioner for Human Rights in the Republic of Tajikistan. The Office was empowered to conduct anti-discrimination assessments of draft laws and regulations, examine complaints of discrimination and resolve disputes through mediation.
11. State funding for the Office has increased annually. Its budget has almost doubled, increasing by 92%, from 1,987,864 somoni in 2022 to 3,821,662 somoni in 2025.
12. In 2023 and 2026, presidential decrees provided for 18 additional posts in the Office. By 2026, the total number of staff had reached 58.

VI. Achievements, best practices, challenges and constraints in the implementation of human rights in the country
A. Cross-cutting issues
1. Equality and non-discrimination
32. Tajikistan regards the principles of equality and non-discrimination as a fundamental element of human rights protection that cuts across all areas of public policy and legislation.
33. Under article 17 of the Constitution, the State guarantees the rights and freedoms of every person, irrespective of ethnicity, race, sex, language, religion, political opinions, education and social or property status.
34. The Equality and Elimination of All Forms of Discrimination Act defines direct and indirect discrimination, establishes a mechanism for conducting anti-discrimination reviews of laws and regulations and grants the right to compensation for material and non-material damage.
35. The Office of the Commissioner for Human Rights has been designated as the competent State authority in this area. A Department for the State Protection of the Right to Equality and Freedom from Discrimination was established within the Office of the Commissioner for Human Rights by a presidential decree of 29 April 2023. The Commissioner has been entrusted with a mandate to conduct anti-discrimination reviews of draft laws and regulations and consider complaints submitted by citizens. Between 2023 and 2025, following the entry into force of the Equality and Elimination of All Forms of Discrimination Act, the Office received 27 complaints relating to discrimination.
36. In February 2026, amendments were introduced to article 7 of the Labour Code explicitly establishing mechanisms for protection against employment discrimination.

2. Right to development
37. Tajikistan regards the right to development as an inalienable human right, realized through the integration of the Sustainable Development Goals into national policy, in accordance with the principle of leaving no one behind.
38. The principal policy document is the National Development Strategy for the period up to 2030, which incorporates the Sustainable Development Goal targets. The National Development Council in the Office of the President coordinates the implementation of the Strategy and the Sustainable Development Goals. The Council has 14 interdepartmental working groups tasked with analysing the implementation of sectoral programmes in the light of long-term goals.
39. A review highlighted the link between national strategies and the Goals: the National Strategy for the Protection of Human Rights for the period up to 2038 is directly aligned with the objectives of the 2030 Agenda.
40. The second voluntary national review, submitted in 2023, showed that the level of integration of the Goal targets into national strategies had reached 78%.
41. Owing to steady economic growth averaging more than 7% annually, the poverty rate fell from 56% in 2010 to less than 20% in recent years.
42. The 2026 labour law reforms, which prohibit discrimination, contribute to the achievement of Goal 8 (decent work and economic growth).
43. The State Programme for the Prevention of Domestic Violence for the period 2025– 2030 was adopted. The expansion of the network of support centres for victims and the increase in the number of applications to the Committee for Women and the Family under the Government of Tajikistan (3,609 complaints in 2025) demonstrate progress in creating a safe environment (Goal 5).
44. The programme on the first 1,000 days of a child’s life is aimed at combating undernutrition. The prevalence of anaemia among children declined from 42% to 35% between 2017 and 2023 (Goals 2 and 3).
45. Vaccination coverage among children has reached 96.7%, which contributes to the goal of reducing child mortality (Goal 3).
46. The transition to 11 years of compulsory education in 2024 contributes to achieving quality education for all (Goal 4).
47. The adoption of the Policy Framework for Inclusive Education for the period up to 2045 is aimed at the integration of children with disabilities (Goal 4.5).
48. Implementation of the National Water Strategy of Tajikistan for the period up to 2040 and the Drinking Water Supply and Sanitation Act is contributing to improved access to drinking water, particularly in urban and rural schools. There has been an improvement in water supply and sanitation conditions in penal institutions (Goal 6).
3. Environmental issues and climate change impacts on vulnerable groups
49. Tajikistan recognizes the inextricable link between the environment and the enjoyment of human rights and establishes the right of its citizens to a healthy environment in law. Given the country’s high vulnerability to climate-related risks, the Government has integrated a human rights approach into its key environmental programmes.
50. In Tajikistan the Environmental Information Act (2011, as amended in 2024) and the Environmental Impact Assessment Act (2017) are in force. In 2018, the procedures for environmental impact assessments were adopted. An Environmental Code is currently being drafted.
51. The National Disaster Risk Reduction Strategy for the period 2019–2034 has been adopted and is being implemented, providing for the creation of a database and the inclusion of data disaggregated by sex, age and disability in hazard/risk vulnerability assessments.

B. Civil and political rights
1. Right to life, liberty and security of person
72. An indefinite moratorium on the use of the death penalty has been in place in Tajikistan since 2004. The death penalty has been replaced by life imprisonment or deprivation of liberty for 25 years. An interdepartmental working group tasked with examining the social and legal aspects of the abolition of the death penalty continues its work within the Executive Office of the President.
73. Under the Code of Criminal Procedure and the Act on Procedures and Conditions for the Custody of Suspects, Accused Persons and Defendants, strict standards apply to the protection of individual rights from the moment of actual detention – namely, the point at which a person is deprived of the freedom to move. The record of arrest must be drawn up within three hours of the person being brought before the competent authorities. Falsifying the time of arrest in the record is considered forgery by an official. Detention without a court decision is limited to 72 hours for adults and 48 hours for minors.
74. A person who has been detained is entitled to a lawyer from the moment of his or her actual detention. Visits with the defence lawyer are granted in private, with no restrictions on their number or duration. The authorities are required to inform the relatives of the detainee’s whereabouts without delay.
75. The Legal Aid Centre of the Ministry of Justice provides free primary and secondary (representation in court) legal assistance to vulnerable groups.
76. The State is implementing a zero-tolerance policy toward torture by improving legislation and law enforcement practices. The definition of torture in article 143 (1) of the Criminal Code has been brought fully into line with article 1 of the Convention against Torture.

77. Before being placed in a temporary holding facility, a medical examination is mandatory and the detainee has the right to have an independent doctor carry out this examination. Where minors lodge complaints of violence, a mandatory medical examination by a panel of at least three doctors who are not subordinate to the institution’s administration is required.

86. Tajikistan became the sixty-seventh country in the world to legally ban corporal punishment of children in all settings in 2024. 87. In 2024, significant amendments were made to the Prevention of Domestic Violence Act, which broadened the definition of violence and established the right of victims to free medical and legal assistance. Between 2023 and 2026, secondary legal assistance was provided to 204 victims of domestic violence.

4. Prohibition of all forms of slavery
112. Tajikistan pursues a consistent policy of eradicating trafficking in persons and forced labour, based on the provisions of the Constitution and its international obligations. 113. An important step was the ratification of the Protocol of 2014 to the Forced Labour Convention, 1930 (No. 29), of the International Labour Organization, which entered into force for the country in 2020 and is aimed at protecting migrants from abusive recruitment practices.

5. Right to privacy and family life
123. Article 23 of the Constitution guarantees the protection of privacy. The unlawful collection or dissemination of information concerning a person’s private life is prohibited by the Criminal Code (art. 144). The Labour Code prohibits employers from requiring employees to provide information concerning their private life (art. 57).
124. The Government places particular emphasis on safeguarding medical confidentiality and privacy in the health sector. Under the Health Code, information concerning an individual’s health status, the results of medical examinations, including HIV status, and reproductive choices is confidential and may not be disclosed without the individual’s consent. Such information is provided only pursuant to a court order or at the request of investigative authorities.
C. Economic, social and cultural rights
1. Right to work and to just and favourable conditions of work
125. The new Equality and Elimination of All Forms of Discrimination Act (2022) established mechanisms for effective protection, including in employment.
126. The new Rights and Social Protection of Persons with Disabilities Act (2026) established guarantees for inclusive employment.
127. In February 2026, amendments were made to the Labour Code (art. 7) that broadened the definition of discrimination, explicitly prohibited sexual harassment and victimization in the workplace and established the right to seek compensation for non-material damage in court for such violations.
128. In 2023, the list of work in which the use of women’s labour is prohibited was reduced by 140 entries (from 334 to 194). Consideration is being given to further reducing the list.
129. As part of the implementation of the State Employment Promotion Programme for the period 2023–2027, around 932,000 new jobs were created between 2022 and 2025.
130. Between 2019 and 2025, a total of 3,164 new enterprises and 53,244 jobs were created as part of the country’s accelerated industrialization efforts.
131. According to data from the Statistics Agency in the Office of the President, the average monthly wage in May 2026 stood at 3,304.05 somoni.
132. To ensure fair remuneration, in 2024, the salaries of public sector employees and law enforcement officers were increased by 40%. Beginning in September 2025, salaries in a number of sectors were increased by up to 30% and further increases are planned for 2026.

2. Right to social security
133. Article 39 of the Constitution guarantees everyone social security in old age and in cases of illness, disability or incapacitation.
134. By 2030, the State plans to increase budget expenditure on social sectors to 16% of gross domestic product (GDP).
135. The Social Protection Development Strategy for the period up to 2040 was approved by a government decision of 29 November 2022. To support its implementation, an action plan for the period 2024–2026 has been adopted with a view to improving quality of life and creating equal opportunities.
136. On 2 January 2025, the Social Entrepreneurship Act came into force, aimed at supporting socially vulnerable groups through job creation and the provision of social services.
137. In 2022, pensions for orphans and persons deprived of parental care were increased by 25%. An agreement with Russia on pension provision (2022) has been ratified for migrant workers, establishing a mechanism for recording length of service and paying contributions.
138. Since 2020, the means-tested targeted social assistance programme has been fully rolled out across all 68 cities and districts of the country. More than 15% of households are covered by the programme. In 2024, more than 419,000 children received targeted assistance payments. A Unified National Information Register is in operation to ensure the accurate registration of beneficiaries. Since January 2024, all statistical reporting in the sector has been converted to electronic format.
139. More than 476 million somoni were allocated to the construction of healthcare and social service facilities during the period 2023–2025.
140. Between 2023 and 2025, the number of day-care centres for children with disabilities and older persons reached 59, with more than 21,000 persons receiving services at these centres. A total of 51.5 million somoni was allocated from the budget for this purpose.
141. The social protection system comprises 115 institutions, including rehabilitation centres, residential care institutions for older persons and a prosthetics and orthotics plant in Dushanbe.
142. The State makes active use of the social procurement mechanism, engaging NGOs to provide support to victims of domestic violence and trafficking in persons.
3. Right to an adequate standard of living
143. During the reporting period, the poverty rate, measured against the national poverty line, fell to 19.8% in 2024, while the poverty rate, measured against the international poverty line, fell to approximately 16.9% in 2024.
144. The Food Safety Programme for the period 2024–2028 has been approved, and the National School Meal Programme already serves more than 450,000 children in 2,000 elementary schools.
145. A significant step forward was the entry into force in 2022 of the new Housing Code, which introduced the concept of social rental arrangements for those in need and strengthened procedural safeguards against unjustified evictions.
146. In the area of water supply, the National Water Strategy for the period up to 2040 is being implemented. In 2025, the State Programme for Drinking Water Supply and Sanitation for the period 2025–2029 was adopted. The country has in force the Water Code, the Drinking Water Supply and Sanitation Act and others.
147. Particular attention was paid in the period 2024–2025 to access to water in educational institutions. The proportion of schools with access to safe drinking water reached 93% in urban areas and 73% in rural areas.
148. In the agricultural sector, which forms the backbone of the economy, the State is working to strengthen women’s land rights. Although women now head 23.5% of dehkan

4. Right to health
149. The Public Health Strategy for the period up to 2030 is being implemented. During the reporting period (2022–2025), funding for the health sector increased steadily. The planned budget allocation for the sector in 2026 amounts to 5.028 billion somoni, representing an increase of 16.1% over 2025 and equivalent to 2.6% of GDP.
150. The legal framework was strengthened through amendments to the Health Code adopted in 2024 and the implementation of programmes on the prevention of noncommunicable diseases and the promotion of reproductive health for 2023–2027.
151. Significant progress has been achieved in maternal and child health. Under-5 mortality fell to 14.6 per 1,000 live births and maternal mortality was 24.0 per 100,000 live births. Thanks to the National Communication Programme Covering the First 1,000 Days of a Child’s Life, the prevalence of stunting among children under 5 years of age decreased from 18% in 2017 to 14% in 2023.
152. To improve access to high-quality services, two modern perinatal centres were opened in Kŭlob and Bokhtar in Khatlon Oblast.

156. Under the National Programme to Counter the Epidemic of HIV/AIDS in the Republic of Tajikistan for the period 2021–2025, HIV Stigma Index studies were conducted and measures to reduce stigma were implemented.
157. In November 2025, the Programme to Counter the Epidemic of HIV and Viral Hepatitis B and C in the Republic of Tajikistan for the period 2026–2030 was approved.
158. In response to issues arising in judicial practice concerning criminal cases involving HIV transmission under article 125 of the Criminal Code, the Plenum of the Supreme Court adopted, on 26 December 2023, a decision on judicial practice in criminal cases involving infection with the human immunodeficiency virus. The decision encourages judicial authorities to take account of international scientific advances in antiretroviral therapy, to give due consideration to defendants’ undetectable viral load status and to assess the actual risk of transmission.
159. With the support of relevant United Nations bodies, a comprehensive gender analysis of the HIV response was conducted, identifying specific barriers faced by women in accessing treatment, prevention services and legal protection.
160. The Ministry of Health and Social Protection, in cooperation with UNICEF, conducted a comprehensive national assessment of the country’s readiness for the elimination of mother-to-child transmission of HIV, syphilis and other infections. The resulting report and the comprehensive action plan developed on its basis are currently under review for approval.
161. Antiretroviral therapy coverage among children living with HIV has increased to 97.6%. Children under 18 living with HIV receive a monthly State benefit equivalent to seven notional units. In 2025, 5 million somoni was allocated for these payments.
162. Significant progress has been made in the prison system, where 100% treatment coverage has been achieved for all identified HIV-positive and tuberculosis patients and the tuberculosis treatment success rate among inmates rose to 93% by 2025.

5. Right to education
163. The National Strategy for the Development of Education for the period up to 2030 remains the key strategic document. The enrolment rate for primary and secondary education in the country exceeds 99.9%. The expansion of access to preschool education continues, with enrolment reaching approximately 30% of children aged 3 to 6 years.
164. In 2024, amendments were made to the Education Act, under which completing full general secondary education (11 years) became compulsory for everyone.
165. The Policy Framework for Inclusive Education for the period up to 2045 and the Rights and Social Protection of Persons with Disabilities Act adopted in June 2026 have strengthened access to quality education for children with special needs and children with disabilities.
166. In 2026, the Programme for the Development of Preschool Education in the Republic of Tajikistan for the period 2026–2030 was approved.
167. The presidential quota scheme continues to operate effectively, enabling over 17,872 young women from remote rural areas to gain admission to higher education institutions. By 2025, the number of female students enrolled in secondary vocational institutions (colleges) had reached 77,360. During the academic years 2020/21 to 2024/25, 3,344 students in teacher-training fields graduated from universities under the presidential quota, including 1,742 women (52.1%).
168. As of 1 September 2025, a modern 10-point grading system has been introduced in all schools across the country. Digital transformation has advanced, with the education management information system improving the collection of disaggregated data on vulnerable groups of children.
169. In 2023 alone, 212 new educational facilities with a total capacity of 45,500 students were opened nationwide.
D. Rights of specific persons or groups
1. Women
170. Gender issues and the promotion of women’s participation in society play a key role in national social policy. Article 17 of the Constitution guarantees equal rights regardless of sex.
171. A gender perspective has been integrated into the National Development Strategy for the period up to 2030. The National Strategy to Promote the Role of Women for the period 2021–2030 is being implemented.
172. The State Programme for the Prevention of Domestic Violence for the period 2025– 2030 was adopted. The Committee for Women and the Family has established the Resource Centre on the Prevention of Domestic Violence and Gender Equality, which operates a helpline (221-40-30). In the first half of 2026, the Centre’s staff provided free legal assistance to 1,011 citizens who sought help regarding domestic violence and other legal matters.
173. According to the 2023 demographic and health survey, the percentage of women who have experienced intimate partner violence has decreased from 31% in 2017 to 16%.
174. In 2022, the Act on State Guarantees of Equal Rights for Men and Women and Equal Opportunities was amended to introduce a modern definition of gender and strengthen the principles of non-discrimination.
175. In 2024, significant amendments were made to the Domestic Violence Act: the definition of violence was broadened and victims’ rights to free medical, legal and social assistance and compensation for damage were clarified.
176. The number of medical rehabilitation rooms for victims has increased from 14 to 27. Assistance is provided free of charge and funded through the State budget.

3. Persons with disabilities
196. As of 2025, there were 172,538 persons with disabilities registered in Tajikistan, of whom 35,381 were children under the age of 18. Social protection is provided through the implementation of laws and programmes, and through the system of pensions, benefits, allowances and tax relief.
197. On 16 June 2026, a new Rights and Social Protection of Persons with Disabilities Act was adopted. The most significant step was the shift from the medical model to the human rights model of disability. The Act established the principles of inclusion, accessibility and reasonable accommodation.
198. The Policy Framework for Inclusive Education for the period up to 2045 and a State programme on accessibility are being implemented. In addition, a system for the early identification and support of children with disabilities is being developed. In 2025, more than 7,300 children with disabilities were attending school.

199. According to the 2020 census, the country is home to 83 nationalities and ethnic groups. To further progress in this area, a national action plan for the period 2024–2027 has been adopted to implement the recommendations of the Committee on the Elimination of Racial Discrimination.
200. The State pays particular attention to the social integration of the Roma ethnic group (Jughi/Lyuli). In recent years, in collaboration with voluntary organizations, assistance has been provided to more than 330 members of this group in obtaining passports and further 25 people have received documents on the basis of court rulings establishing the fact of their birth. Roma children attend general education schools on an equal footing with other children, and 1,083 Roma children are currently enrolled in school.
201. During the 2025/26 school year, schools and classes providing instruction in Uzbek, Russian, Kyrgyz, Turkmen and English are in operation throughout the country. There are 41 registered voluntary associations of ethnic minorities in the country.

5. Migrants, refugees, asylum-seekers and internally displaced persons
202. Tajikistan is a leader in Central Asia on refugee protection, having been the first country in the region to ratify the 1951 Convention relating to the Status of Refugees and the 1967 Protocol thereto in 1993. The legal status of refugees is governed by the Refugees Act and a number of regulations, which ensure access to education, healthcare and the labour market.
203. As of late 2024, there were 10,753 registered refugees and asylum-seekers (2,670 families) in the country, 63% of whom were Afghan nationals.
204. An important legislative reform was the repeal of the provision in article 499 of the Code of Administrative Offences on the mandatory expulsion of refugees for violations of residence regulations, thereby strengthening adherence to the principle of non-refoulement.
205. To provide temporary housing, there are accommodation centres near Dushanbe and a new facility in Jaloliddin Balkhi District in Khatlon Oblast with a capacity for 100 families.
206. In the field of labour migration, the Strategy for the Regulation of Migration Processes for the period up to 2040 is being implemented.
207. Particular attention is paid to the families of migrant workers who remain in Tajikistan: in partnership with UNICEF and IOM, projects have been implemented to support more than 400 vulnerable women and children, providing them with healthcare and access to social services. The Ministry of Internal Affairs and relevant departments operate hotlines to provide immediate assistance to migrants who find themselves in difficult situations abroad.
6. Stateless persons
208. A landmark achievement in this area was the adoption in December 2019 of the Act on Amnesty in Connection with the Regularization of the Legal Status of Foreign Nationals and Stateless Persons Staying Unlawfully in Tajikistan, which remained in effect until 25 December 2022. As a result of the implementation of this law, 1,421 people were granted residence permits, of whom 952 were stateless persons (including 635 women).
209. Since 2014, the Government has been undertaking a large-scale effort to document the population, in collaboration with the Office of the United Nations High Commissioner for Refugees. During the period from 2014 to February 2023, 59,287 persons with undetermined or no citizenship were identified in the country, of whom 55,416 have already been issued with identity documents. Assistance in obtaining a Tajik passport was provided to 35,166 people, 84% of whom were women, primarily residing in remote rural areas. The main causes of statelessness remain a lack of legal literacy, financial difficulties and living far from administrative centres.
210. Tajikistan is currently considering whether to accede to the 1954 Convention relating to the Status of Stateless Persons and the 1961 Convention on the Reduction of Statelessness.
211. Although the amnesty law to legalize stateless persons and foreign nationals staying unlawfully in Tajikistan has expired, stateless persons (originally from the former Soviet Union) retain the right to obtain a residence permit under the current rules governing the stay of foreign nationals.
