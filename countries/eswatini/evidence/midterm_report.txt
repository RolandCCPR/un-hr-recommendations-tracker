METHODOLOGY I. Following the adoption of Eswatini's third-cycle Universal Periodic Review (UPR) Recommendations in March 2022, the National Mechanism for Reporting and Follow-Up (NMRF) undertook a collaborative and consultative process to develop the UPR Implementation Plan (UPR IP) 2023-2026. This process involved meaningful engagement with key stakeholders to ensure alignment with national priorities and the international obligations. 2. On October 21 and 22, 2024, the NMRF, in partnership with the Office of the High Commissioner for Human Rights (OHCHR) and the United Nations Country Team (UNCT), convened a workshop to enhance the implementation of UPR recommendations. I The workshop provided a platform for duty bearers to present updates on progress in implementing the UPR IP activities, fostering a shared understanding of achievements and challenges. 3. Following this engagement, the NMRF identified thematic focus areas for the preparation of Eswatini's UPR Mid-Term Report. The focus areas include inclusive education, access to safe drinking water and sanitation, and combatting gender-based violence. To gather comprehensive data, additional information was requested from the Ministry of Education and Training, the Ministry of Natural Resources and Energy and the Deputy Prime Minister's Office, between November and December 2024. The criteria used to select the identified thematic areas was premised on the fact that the Ministries leading the implementation of the recommendations had readily available information. 4. On January 14 and 15, 2025, the initial draft of the UPR Mid-Term Report was compiled, incorporating inputs from the aforementioned stakeholders. To ensure inclusivity and accuracy, a validation session was held on February 13, 2025, with the participation of government departments, ministries, the Commission on Human Rights and Public Administration (Eswatini's NHRI), civil society organizations, the Swaziland Council of Churches, and other relevant partners. 1 List of stakeholders that participated is attached as Annexure I. 3

The mid-term report provides an overview of the progress made by Eswatini in implementing some recommendations adopted in 2022. It highlights the efforts undertaken by the country to improve the human rights situation in the period 2022-2024 focusing on the thematic areas of education, access to safe drinking water and sanitation, and combatting gender-based violence. Achievements: 7. Legal, Policy, and Institutional Reforms: Since the previous review, there have been significant strides made by the country in strengthening its legal and policy framework for human rights. The significant reforms include the adoption of the Sexual Offences and Domestic Violence (SODV) Regulations in 2022, addressing Gender Based Violence (GB V) issues to ensure the holistic implementation of the SODV Act 2018. Furthermore, the National Strategy on Ending Violence (2023-2027) was adopted to mainstream gender issues in the National Development Plan. The country revised the National Gender Policy (2023-2033) which outlines the country's vision for gender equality and provides guidance and tools for stakeholders and institutions in all sectors to mainstream gender and advance gender equality and equity. 8. The Department of Gender and Family Issues (DGFI), in collaboration with Civil Society Organizations, has been actively working to raise public awareness about the Sexual Offences and Domestic Violence (SODV) Act through various initiatives. These efforts include conducting awareness campaigns through radio, television, organizing bus rank GBV prevention roadshows, and participating in national and social events to educate the public about GBV/VAC issues. Additionally, training workshops for law enforcement officers, Prosecutors, healthcare providers, and other stakeholders have been conducted to ensure effective implementation of the Act. There were Community engagement programs, such as the GB V symposium and community dialogues, which have further enhanced the understanding and reporting of GBV incidents. 4

Eswatini made significant strides in enacting the Sexual Offences and Domestic Violence (SODV) Act(2018), which makes provisions concerning sexual offences and domestic violence, prevention and the protection of all persons from harm and acts of domestic violence amongst others. It further ensures justice for victims or survivors of all forms of violence. 17. To ensure holistic implementation of this Act, SODV Regulations were adopted in 2022. Pursuant to adopting these Regulations, stakeholders including Police Officers, Prosecutors, Social Workers, and Judicial Officers have undertaken continuous capacity- strengthening initiatives on the country's legal framework and standards for handling GBV cases. These trainings have led to improved handling of cases under SODV Act, as the functionaries have a clear understanding of the law and apply it consistently. These initiatives were a result of collaboration and support from the Development Partners as well as Civil Society Organisations. Policies and strategies 18. In 2023, the country revised the National Gender Policy which covers a 10-year period (2023-2033). The revised policy outlines the country's vision for gender equality and provides guidance and tools for stakeholders and institutions in all sectors to enhance mainstreaming of gender and advance gender equality and equity, reduce gender stereotypes and discriminatory practices in all spheres of life including health, education, employment, agriculture and politics. 19. To promote safe school environments, pilot schools at secondary level were trained on the nine pillars that are a building block for provision of a safe and protective environment. The nine pillars are: rights-based based socially inclusive school policies and practices, Quality, relevant, inclusive curriculum, teacher development, Enabling and inclusive infrastructure, material support, Nutrition and Health Promotion, Social Welfare services including psychosocial support, parental involvement and Leadership and Coordination. The Care and Support for Teaching and Learning (CSTL) program was piloted in 20 secondary schools and is now being rolled out to all secondary schools. The Unit collaborated with civil society organizations to address issues of violence, particularly school-related gender-based violence, through various programs. IV. NEEDS FOR CAPACITY BUILDING AND TECHNICAL ASSISTANCE 66. The Government of Eswatini is committed to continuing the implementation of the accepted recommendations to meet both domestic and international obligations. To achieve this, Eswatini seeks technical assistance and capacity building in the following key areas: o Strengthening the information management capacity of the NMRF through capacity-building initiatives and the rollout of the National Reporting Tracking Database, to ensure an improved tracking, recording, and reporting on UPR and other human rights mechanisms' recommendations. Alignment of legislation to the constitution and international standards as well as o developing new Pieces of legislation, domesticating treaties on human rights ratified by Eswatini. 22
