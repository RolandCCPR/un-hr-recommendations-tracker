# Suriname — Implementation of 2021 (3rd cycle) UPR Recommendations

Assessment of Suriname's implementation of the 147 recommendations received in the 3rd UPR cycle (A/HRC/49/6, 39th session, 2021-11-01), using the Human Rights Committee A–E grading system (CCPR/C/108/2). Evidence is the 4th-cycle pre-sessional documentation (November 2026 (session 53) review).

**Sources**

- **National Report** — A/HRC/WG.6/53/SUR/1
- **UN Compilation** — A/HRC/WG.6/53/SUR/2, OHCHR
- **Stakeholders' Summary** — A/HRC/WG.6/53/SUR/3, OHCHR

Grades: **A** largely satisfactory · **B** partially satisfactory · **C** not satisfactory / no relevant action · **D** no information · **E** measures contrary to, or rejection of, the recommendation.

This is a **first-pass assessment for expert review**. Recommendations are grouped by their OHCHR theme; within a theme, near-identical asks share a grade, with separate blocks where the substance (and grade) differ. Each block notes the position Suriname took at the review (supported / noted / supported-noted); a *noted* recommendation was not accepted.

## Summary

| Grade | Count | Share |
|---|---:|---:|
| **A** — Largely implemented / action largely satisfactory | 14 | 10% |
| **B** — Partially implemented / steps taken, further action needed | 85 | 58% |
| **C** — Not implemented / no relevant action taken | 39 | 27% |
| **D** — No information available on implementation | 9 | 6% |
| **Total** | 147 | 100% |

**Partial implementation (B)** is the dominant outcome, and Suriname gives a detailed National Report: it acceded to the Convention against Torture (16 November 2021) and abolished the death penalty in its Military Criminal Code (2021, **A**), adopted the Equal Treatment in Employment Act and the Act on Violence and Sexual Harassment in Employment (2022), the Children's Ombudsperson Act (2025), a National Social Protection Strategy 2024-2030, a National Education Policy Plan 2024-2031, a Multilingual Education Programme, a National Policy Plan for Persons with Disabilities 2025-2030, a national FPIC protocol (2025) and a Land Rights Project, and introduced the General Act for Persons with Disabilities in Parliament. **No relevant action (C)** persists on the remaining optional protocols (OP-CAT, OP-CEDAW, OP-CRPD, OP-ICESCR, the Second Optional Protocol), ICPPED, the national human rights institution (draft Act only), legislation on sexual orientation and gender identity, the minimum age of marriage, corporal punishment, child labour, a standing invitation to special procedures and the Collective Rights of Indigenous and Tribal Peoples Act (still a draft).
