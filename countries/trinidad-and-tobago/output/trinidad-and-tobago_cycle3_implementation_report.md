# Trinidad and Tobago — Implementation of 2021 (3rd cycle) UPR Recommendations

Assessment of Trinidad and Tobago's implementation of the 166 recommendations received in the 3rd UPR cycle (A/HRC/49/16, 39th session, 2021-11-09), using the Human Rights Committee A–E grading system (CCPR/C/108/2). Evidence is the 4th-cycle pre-sessional documentation (November 2026 (session 53) review).

**Sources**

- The **National Report** (A/HRC/WG.6/53/TTO/1) was not yet available at the time of assessment.
- **UN Compilation** — A/HRC/WG.6/53/TTO/2, OHCHR
- **Stakeholders' Summary** — A/HRC/WG.6/53/TTO/3, OHCHR

Grades: **A** largely satisfactory · **B** partially satisfactory · **C** not satisfactory / no relevant action · **D** no information · **E** measures contrary to, or rejection of, the recommendation.

This is a **first-pass assessment for expert review**. Recommendations are grouped by their OHCHR theme; within a theme, near-identical asks share a grade, with separate blocks where the substance (and grade) differ. Each block notes the position Trinidad and Tobago took at the review (supported / noted / supported-noted); a *noted* recommendation was not accepted.

## Summary

| Grade | Count | Share |
|---|---:|---:|
| **B** — Partially implemented / steps taken, further action needed | 62 | 37% |
| **C** — Not implemented / no relevant action taken | 88 | 53% |
| **D** — No information available on implementation | 14 | 8% |
| **E** — Contrary measures taken, or recommendation rejected | 2 | 1% |
| **Total** | 166 | 100% |

**No relevant action (C)** is the dominant outcome, with a thin evidence base on the State's own account because its National Report was not yet available (14 recommendations are **D**). **Partial implementation (B)** appears where the documents record concrete measures: the 2023 National Strategic Action Plan on gender-based and sexual violence, anti-trafficking plans, shelters and a first conviction, a National Climate Change Policy, near-universal free education and a comprehensive social protection system. **No relevant action (C)** persists on the mandatory death penalty (48 people under sentence of death in 2026, no moratorium), treaty ratification, refugee legislation, an anti-discrimination law, a national human rights institution and prison conditions. There is a clear regression (**E**): consensual same-sex relations were re-criminalised in 2025 when the Court of Appeal reinstated sections 13 and 16 of the Sexual Offences Act, and repeated States of Emergency since 2024 have expanded police powers.

## Refugees & asylum seekers

### Recommendations 109.90-109.92, 109.94-109.96, 109.98, 109.100, 109.101 (Enact refugee legislation and an asylum procedure)

*9 of 166 — position: 9 noted.*

**Grade: C — Information/action not satisfactory**

**Evidence:**
- UN Compilation (A/HRC/WG.6/53/TTO/2), para. 53 — Human Rights Committee: "the absence of a comprehensive legislative and institutional framework for the protection of refugees and asylum-seekers"
- UN Compilation (A/HRC/WG.6/53/TTO/2), para. 52 — United Nations experts: "the High Court ruling declaring the Convention relating to the Status of Refugees inapplicable in Trinidad and Tobago"
- UN Compilation (A/HRC/WG.6/53/TTO/2), para. 51 — United Nations country team: "progress on the regularization of and school access for migrants, refugees and asylum-seekers following an updated refugee policy"

**Assessment:** There is still no refugee legislation or status-determination procedure; a High Court ruling declared the Refugee Convention inapplicable (2023); an updated refugee policy and a 2026 Migration Registration Framework offer only partial progress.

### Recommendations 109.97, 109.99 (Protect asylum-seekers and migrants)

*2 of 166 — position: 2 noted.*

**Grade: C — Information/action not satisfactory**

**Evidence:**
- UN Compilation (A/HRC/WG.6/53/TTO/2), para. 53 — Human Rights Committee: "increasing numbers of deportations of individuals seeking international protection and harmful detention practices affecting adults and children"

**Assessment:** No refugee legislation exists; UN experts warned of deportations and the Human Rights Committee of harmful detention practices.
