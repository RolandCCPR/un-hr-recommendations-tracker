I. Introduction
1. The United Republic of Tanzania (URT) has maintained sustained commitment to human rights through active engagement in the Universal Periodic Review Mechanism (UPR). During the 3rd cycle (2021), the URT accepted 187 recommendations with 20 being partial acceptance. The Government of the URT is pleased to submit its 4th National Report under the UPR mechanism reflecting how implementation of the recommendations contributed to promotion, protection and realisation of civil, political, economic, social and cultural rights in Mainland Tanzania and Zanzibar over the four-year reporting period.
II. Methodology
2. The National report was prepared through a consultative process coordinated by the Ministry of Constitutional and Legal Affairs in Mainland Tanzania and the President's Office, Constitution, Legal Affairs, Public Service and Good Governance in Zanzibar.
3. Data was collected using standardized reporting templates from State actors, including Ministries, Departments and Agencies (MDAs) and the Judiciary. Inputs were also obtained from the National Human Rights Institution, Civil Society Organizations (CSOs), FaithBased and Disability-Based Organizations (FBOs and DBOs) from Mainland Tanzania and Zanzibar. The report was validated through inter-ministerial and stakeholder consultations to ensure compliance with UPR reporting requirements.1
III. New developments since the last review
Democratic governance
4. A Task-Force on Multi-Party Democracy was established by Her Excellency the President on 23 December 2021 with the objective of advising the Government on how to enhance civic space. The Task-Force collected views from stakeholders including political parties, civil society and the media and submitted its recommendations in October 2022. Its recommendations led to lifting the ban on political party rallies.
5. The governance philosophy of the Government was also developed by Her Excellency the President in 2022 being, “Reconciliation, Resilience, Reforms and Rebuilding.” These 4 pillars are the guiding principles that led to revoking the suspension of 4 major independent newspapers in February 20222.
6. The administration of Criminal Justice was subjected to review through by establishment of a Presidential Criminal Justice Reform Commission launched on 6 January, 2023. The recommendations are at various stages of implementation.
7. Further, Local Government Elections were held in 2024 and General Elections in October, 2025. In the spirit of participatory governance, a Presidential Commission of Inquiry was established on 18 November, 2025 to examine the unrest that occurred during and after the General Elections. In implementing its recommendations, a Criminal Investigation Commission was established on 18 May, 2026 to address the criminal aspects of the unrest.
Human rights mainstreaming in development processes
8. The Tanzania Housing and Population census was conducted in 2022 as a tool for allocating resources equitably for socio-economic rights. The results revealed total population reached 61,741,120 representing a 3.2% increase from the 2012 census. Mainland Tanzania with a total of 59,851,347 persons (29,137,638 males and 30,713,709 females) and Zanzibar a total of 1,889,773 persons (915,492 males and 974,281 females). The total population aged 7 years and above having some form of disability reached 5.3 million persons which is 11.2% of the total population.

9. On 17 July, 2026 the National Development Agenda Vision 2050 was officially launched. The URT practices a human rights approach to development as Vision 2050 envisions “a prosperous, inclusive and equitable society grounded in good governance, respect for human dignity, and sustainable development.” Vision 2050 has integrated the human-rights orientated Sustainable Development Goals (SDG) by mainstreaming targets focusing on poverty reduction, quality education, health, gender equality, clean energy, industrialization, and environmental sustainability.
Strategic investments and economic transformation
10. The URT accelerated structural transformation through strategic infrastructure and productive investments to expand economic opportunities and improve living standards. Construction of the Standard Gauge Railway (SGR) progressed to approximately 2,561 km, with 722 km operational and over 6,000,000 passengers transported.3 Electricity generation capacity increased to 4,522.54 MW, including commissioning of the 2,115 MW Julius Nyerere Hydropower Project, while electricity access expanded to all 12,318 villages and national electrification reached 79.5%.
11. Public investment in irrigation expanded irrigated land to 983,466 hectares, and investment promotion reforms resulted in 526 registered investment projects in 2023. Between 2022 and 2026, the economy generated 4,263,021 jobs across agriculture, construction, industry and services. Budget self-reliance increased to 78.0% in 2023/24 and the URT received a record in Foreign Direct Investments (FDI) in 2025 as the country attracted nearly $11 billion across more than 900 registered projects.

B. Economic, social and cultural rights
Right to development and elimination of poverty Recommendations: 145.7, 145.16, 145.22, 145.26, 145.60, 145.62, 145.63
65. The URT successfully concluded implementation of Tanzania Development Vision 2025 through the Third National Five-Year Development Plan (2021/22–2025/26), contributing to sustained economic growth and improved living standards. Gross Domestic Product (GDP) increased from 4.7 per cent in 2021 to 5.9 per cent in 2025, while GDP at 2019 prices rose from TZS 211.975 trillion (USD 81.2 billion) in 2024 to TZS 234.104 trillion (USD 91.81 billion) in 2025. GDP per capita increased by 7.4 per cent to TZS 3,544,124 (USD 1,390), export earnings grew from USD 6.4 billion in 2021 to USD 10.6 billion in 2025, and inflation remained within the medium-term target at 3.3%.
66. Poverty reduction and economic empowerment continued to be addressed including through sustained economic growth and productive sector investments. The 2025 Integrated Household Budget Survey shows that the national basic needs poverty rate declined from 26.4% in 2020 to 24.9% in 2025. GDP growth increased from 4.7% in 2022 and 5.5% in 2024, and is projected to reach 5.9–6.0% in 2026, supported by growth in agriculture, infrastructure, mining, and financial services.
67. Agriculture remained a key driver of poverty reduction, employment, and economic transformation. Between 2022 and 2026, the sector employed 61.4% of the national workforce, contributed 26.3% of GDP, and maintained annual growth of 4.0–4.1%. V. Challenges during implementation which require support
109. Although the URT has made significant progress in the promotion and protection of human rights, challenges have been identified which require support. These are fundamental to enforcing the foundation for the realisation of human rights. The Government of the United Republic of Tanzania is requesting technical and financial support to:
(a) Finalise the development of a National Human Rights Awareness Program on Rights and Duties targeting both duty bearers and rights holders;
(b) Support in translating laws from the English Language to Swahili; and
(c) Build the capacity of government officials on how to harness technology to accelerate development in the various sectors.
