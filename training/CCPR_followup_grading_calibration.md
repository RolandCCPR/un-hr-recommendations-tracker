# Human Rights Committee follow‑up grading — calibration reference

Derived from the Committee's own follow‑up assessment reports
(`CCPR/C/138/2/Add.1–4`, `CCPR/C/141/2/Add.1–4`, `CCPR/C/144/2/Add.1–2`,
`CCPR/C/145/2/Add.1–3`) — 13 country evaluations, ~40 follow‑up paragraphs,
~60 sub‑graded limbs. Purpose: know when the Rapporteur gives **A / B / C / D / E**.

The five definitions (CCPR/C/108/2, para. 21):

| | |
|---|---|
| **A** | Reply/action **largely satisfactory** — evidence of *significant action* toward implementation. |
| **B** | Reply/action **partially satisfactory** — *steps taken*, but additional information or action still needed. |
| **C** | Reply/action **not satisfactory** — a response was received, but the action/information is *not relevant or does not implement* the recommendation. |
| **D** | **No cooperation** — no follow‑up report received after the reminder(s). |
| **E** | Measures taken are **contrary to, or reflect rejection of**, the recommendation. |

---

## The operative test (what the Rapporteur actually asks)

For each recommendation (and each lettered sub‑limb), in order:

1. **Was a follow‑up report received at all?** No → **D**. (D is *only* total non‑reporting. A report that omits a sub‑limb is **C** for that limb, not D — see Portugal 33(c): "No information was provided" → C.)
2. **Did the State take contrary measures, defend the impugned measure, or reject the recommendation — across the paragraph, with no remedial step?** Yes → **E**.
3. **Did the State take a concrete step aimed at *this specific ask*** (law adopted, bill with real content submitted, system launched, training delivered with figures, resources hired, releases effected)?
   - **No** — only the existing legal framework restated / generic assurances / a draft "being prepared" / activity that doesn't reach the recommended outcome → **C**.
   - **Yes, and there is evidence it is operational / producing results, and the core ask is met** → **A**.
   - **Yes, but** implementation/impact/outcome data is missing, **or** the measure is adopted‑but‑not‑in‑force, **or** only part of a multi‑limb ask is covered, **or** the step stops short of the specific thing asked → **B**.

The grade attaches to **the specific ask**, judged over **the reporting period** — not to whether the underlying right improved in general.

---

## A — largely satisfactory  *(rare; ~10% of limbs in the sample)*

Give **A** when the State did the recommended thing **and** shows it is real:

- **Adopted‑and‑applied legislation with results.** Portugal 41 [A]: Act No. 9/2020 adopted *and applied* — 2,000+ pre‑trial detainees released — plus 320 judges/prosecutors and 100 inspectors hired. Follow‑up questions are refinements ("will it be extended beyond the pandemic?"), not a reiteration.
- **A concrete deliverable + quantified activity directly on a bounded ask.** Portugal 33(b) [A]: child‑trafficking protocol *developed* + training delivered with numbers (80 inspectors, 100 professionals). Committee "welcomes the development of the protocol and the information on the training programmes".
- **Concrete protective action + durable follow‑through on a bounded ask.** Germany 31(f) [A]: COVID measures for older persons taken + national health reserve established + "lessons‑learned" process.

Signals: "**The Committee welcomes the steps taken** …"; the only asks are for *additional* statistics or future‑proofing; **no** "reiterates its recommendation".

**A is not:** a good legal framework that pre‑dated the CO; an adopted law not yet in force; a plan without results.

---

## B — partially satisfactory  *(the default; ~55–60% of limbs)*

Give **B** when the State took **a genuine step toward this ask** but it is incomplete. The recurring fact‑patterns:

- **Law adopted and in force, but no implementation/impact data.** Armenia 16 [B]: domestic‑violence law amended and in force (July 2024) + data system launched — but "lack of concrete information, including statistical data, on the means of redress". Germany 21 [B], 43 [B]: Acts in force, review under way — "lack of concrete information on … practical implementation".
- **Law/reform adopted but NOT YET IN FORCE.** Botswana 30 [B]: Refugees Act 2024 adopted (UNHCR‑involved) "has not yet entered into force" → "reiterates its recommendation that the State take all measures to ensure the prompt entry into force".
- **A substantive reform bill *submitted to parliament*, plus partial administrative measures.** Armenia 42 [B]: electoral‑reform package submitted Sept 2024 + a CEC decision on polling‑station accessibility. (Contrast: a bill merely "being prepared / awaiting approval" → C.)
- **Legislative reform done, but practice still deficient.** Armenia 40(b)–(e) [B]: Police Guard law + assembly‑law amendments + training — but "continued disproportionate use of force" reported. Botswana 22 [B], 26 [B].
- **Most of a multi‑limb ask covered with concrete measures; one element unevidenced.** Portugal 33(a) [B], 37 [B].
- **Concrete training on a narrow "provide training" ask, but no content/impact figures.** Lao 20(e) [B] — even though the rest of para 20 is C.

Signals: "**The Committee welcomes … / notes with satisfaction …**" **followed by** "**it regrets, however, the lack of … / the absence of specific data on …**" and "**reiterates its recommendation**" or a request for the missing outcome data. Recommended action is normally still *discontinuation* — B does **not** mean the procedure stays open.

---

## C — not satisfactory  *(~30% of limbs)*

Give **C** when a reply came but it does **not implement** the ask. Markers, any of which suffice:

- **"Absence of / lack of details on concrete measures taken during the reporting period."** (Cambodia 9, 35, 39 — all [C].)
- **The State restates the constitutional/legal framework or issues generic assurances / denials** as if that answers the recommendation. Cambodia 39: "authorities are not legally allowed to harass … the law protects …". Lao 20(a): "The Government does not allow any individual … to force them to disappear."
- **Draft legislation only** — "being prepared", "submitted for consultation", "awaiting approval" — with nothing operational alongside it. Cambodia 9, 35.
- **A legislative step that deliberately stops short of the specific thing asked.** Germany 31(d) [C]: guardianship law reformed, "but the legislation still provides for circumstances under which the forced sterilization of adults with disabilities remains permissible" — and removing that exception was the ask.
- **Formally adequate law, but credible unrebutted reports of the opposite in practice, and no remedial action.** Lao 40 [C]: consultation/compensation laws "welcomed", but reports of increased land‑grabbing and continued persecution of the Hmong.
- **Activity described but the recommended *outcome* (accountability, convictions, reparation) not shown.** Armenia 40(a) [C]: investigations described, "but … cases still pending or concluded with no consequences … lack of information on compensation".
- **A sub‑limb not addressed at all.** Portugal 33(c) [C].

Signal: "**While noting the information provided, the Committee regrets … It reiterates its recommendations.**" — with **no** "welcomes concrete steps".

---

## D — no cooperation

Only when **no follow‑up report is submitted** after the reminder(s). None of the 13 sampled States were at D (all engaged). Do not use D for a thin or evasive reply — that is C.

---

## E — contrary / rejection  *(very rare; high bar)*

Reserve **E** for a whole paragraph where the totality is: **the State affirms or defends the impugned measure, and/or the situation has regressed, and no remedial step was taken.**

- Lao 38 [E]: ask was to remove disenfranchisement of persons with disabilities and prisoners and give effect to genuine political participation. The State's reply **reaffirmed** that "persons with mental disorders, persons … convicted … and persons … deprived of their liberty are not eligible to vote", NGOs reported **further** suppression of civic space and another one‑party‑controlled election, and **no** legislative measure was taken. → contrary + retrogression + inaction, paragraph‑wide.

The Committee is conservative here. It stayed at **C**, not E, even where:
- the State **defended keeping** a criminal‑defamation offence the CO said to abolish (Cambodia 35(c));
- civil‑society actors were **jailed** for exposing corruption during the reporting period (Cambodia 9).

So E needs the State's own position/measures to run against the recommendation *and* generally an absence of any offsetting step — not merely a bad situation on the ground (that is C).

---

## Cross‑cutting observations

- **Sub‑grading is normal and expected.** Report a paragraph as e.g. `31 [B][C][A]`. Grade each lettered limb on its own facts; the paragraph has no single "overall" grade.
- **Reporting‑period focus.** "No concrete measures taken *during the reporting period*" is decisive for C even if the framework is otherwise fine.
- **"Reiterates its recommendation"** appears in both B and C evaluations; the distinguishing feature is whether the Committee first **"welcomes concrete steps"** (B) or not (C).
- **Recommended action is almost always "discontinuation of the follow‑up procedure"** in current practice (2023–2026 sample: 13/13), with the outstanding points folded into the next periodic report. The grade — not continuation — is the substantive output.
- **The "no NGO information" effect.** With no civil‑society submission the Rapporteur has less to test the State's account against, and grades tend to sit slightly higher / rest on the State's own framing (Germany, Portugal, Ukraine, Lao para‑by‑para). A concrete completed State measure on a bounded ask can still reach **A** on State information alone (Portugal 41, 33(b); Germany 31(f)). A thin State reply with no NGO rebuttal still lands **C** where it only restates the framework (Ukraine 48).

## Applying this to the UPR assessment in this project

Our UPR grading uses the same A–E letters. Adjustments to align with Committee practice:

1. **Split multi‑part asks** and grade each part, rather than one grade per thematic cluster.
2. **Be stricter on A.** Reserve it for a *completed* measure with evidence it is operating.
   - Namibia "free tertiary education *from 2026*" → future measure → **B**, not A.
   - Denmark ratification of the Enforced Disappearance Convention (done, 2022, first report submitted) → **A** stands.
   - Namibia ILO C190 ratified (Aug 2025) → **A** stands (ratification is the whole ask).
3. **Adopted‑but‑not‑in‑force = B**, never A (matches Botswana 30).
4. **"Bill pending / under consideration for years" = C**; **"substantive bill submitted to parliament + partial measures" = B** (matches Armenia 42 vs Cambodia 9).
5. **Keep E rare.** Use it only where the State's own measures/position run against the recommendation (US federal death‑penalty reinstatement; Denmark asylum externalisation legislation; Paraguay "Garrote" NGO law) — not merely where the situation is bad.
6. A **thin Stakeholders' Summary entry** on a theme pulls the grade toward the State's account — note this as a confidence caveat, as the Committee's practice implies.

## Corrected B/C threshold (from the full-corpus analysis + blind-test result)

*Added after mining all ~800 graded sub-limbs (`CCPR_followup_grading_corpus_analysis.md`) and a
24-item blind test. The blind test scored 18/24 — and **every one of the 6 misses was one grade
too strict** (5× graded C where the Committee gave B; 1× B where it gave A). The corpus explains
why: B and C are each ~43% of all grades ever given (86% together), so the whole system turns on
the B/C line, and the working "C = does not implement" test was drawn too wide.*

**C is narrower than "the recommendation is not yet implemented."** The Committee's C language is
"**regrets** that [no concrete action / no information / not implemented] … **reiterates its
recommendation**". Every genuine C in the corpus is one of: *no information on that limb*, *measures
that pre-date the concluding observations*, *only "under study" / a directive to review / generic
assurances*, or *a bill pending for years with nothing new*.

**B is "welcomes / notes [a concrete step] … but requires additional information."** If, within the
review period, the State **enacted, adopted, amended, launched or formally introduced** a measure
**on the specific ask** — even if it is not in force, has no impact data yet, or covers only part
of the ask — that is **B**, not C.

### Operative test for the UPR grading

Grade each split sub-ask:

- **D** — the evidence base says nothing on this sub-ask at all.
- **E** — the State refuses / defends the impugned measure, **or** a new contrary law or measure was
  adopted in the period (regression). Keep rare.
- **C** — a response exists but: only pre-existing or ongoing measures; only "under consideration" /
  "internal analysis" / a directive to review; a bill stalled for years or rejected by the
  legislature; or the measures named are off-point for this recommendation.
- **B** — at least one concrete, on-point measure was **adopted / enacted / amended / launched /
  formally introduced during the review period**, and the ask is not yet essentially met
  (not in force, no results yet, partial, one of several limbs). This includes a substantive bill
  freshly introduced and moving.
- **A** — a concrete on-point measure was completed and the core of the ask is essentially met
  (ratification done; law in force; the thing asked for exists), even if refinements remain or
  implementation data is still requested.

### Recalibration actually applied to the four country files

- **Denmark**: 4 clusters C→B — outstanding-treaty ratifications (ICPPED 2022, ILO C190 2024),
  immigration-detention practice, conditions of detention (Sept-2023 disciplinary reform with a
  measured drop in >14-day solitary), racial discrimination (Feb-2025 national action plan).
- **Namibia**: 15 clusters C→B — where a dated measure lands in the period (Marriage Act 2024,
  Access to Information Act 2022, National Disability Council Act amended 2025, Social Protection
  Policy 2021–2030, Sanitation & Hygiene Strategy 2022–2027, revised National Housing Policy 2023,
  Ombudsman Bill drafted/tabled 2025, the active Torture Bill process, legal-aid threshold rise +
  court expansion, Food and Nutrition Security Policy revised 2021, old-age pension increase).
  Kept C where only a directive/consultation/pending draft exists (comprehensive anti-discrimination
  law, NHRAP successor, data-protection bills, standing invitation, Indigenous-Peoples recognition,
  business-and-human-rights dialogue).
- **Paraguay**: 12 clusters C→B — judicial digitalisation, 100% "Zero Hunger in Schools" coverage,
  2025 ratifications, the new women's prison + 2024 alternatives-to-detention protocol, the National
  Plan for Indigenous Peoples with 637 consultations and IACtHR-judgment compliance, disability
  certification reform, 2023–24 torture-monitoring protocols, Indigenous Languages Act 7008/22,
  the online birth-registration system, land acquired under IACtHR judgments, partial HR-education
  plan rollout. Kept C for the long-stalled asks (anti-discrimination law, migration law, abortion,
  criadazgo, minimum marriage age, journalist-protection bill) and the regressions stay E
  (Garrote law, Resolution 933).
- **United States**: unchanged. The evidence base shows inaction plus large-scale 2025 regression,
  which already maps correctly to C and E — the over-strict B/C error did not arise here.
